05  —  The Experiments

What I tried

The analysis, told as the questions it answered in order — from "can a straight line do it?" to "which factor is the model leaning on?"

05.1Experiment 1 — A linear baseline

Question. How far does a simple linear classifier get on its own?

Setup. Logistic Regression on the four standardised composites, five-fold stratified CV.

.879
accuracy
.952
ROC-AUC

Reading. A linear boundary already separates the classes well. That tells us the predictor–burnout relationships are strong and roughly monotonic — the signal is not hiding in exotic interactions. It also sets the bar that the nonlinear models have to clear.

05.2Experiment 2 — Does nonlinearity help?

Question. Do kernel and ensemble models beat the baseline by enough to matter?

Reading. Random Forest is best on every metric; XGBoost is a close second. The convergence of four very different algorithms on the same conclusion is itself reassuring — the result is not an artefact of one model’s quirks. Full metrics are in Results §6.5.

05.3Experiment 3 — Which predictors is the model using?

Question. When Random Forest classifies a student, which of the four factors is doing the work?

Reading. Psychological distress dominates. Social support and self-efficacy are the next most-used, both protective. Academic stress — on its own strongly correlated with burnout (r = .441) — barely moves the model once distress is available, because the two overlap heavily (r = .683).

05.4Experiment 4 — Robustness

Some robustness is built into the design; some important checks were not done and are the clearest next steps.

Checks in place

  • Stratified folds preserve the class ratio in every split.
  • Scaling fit on training folds only — no preprocessing leakage.
  • Every observation is tested exactly once; mean and SD across folds reported.
  • Four algorithms of different families agree on the ranking of predictors.
  • ML feature ranking matches the independent regression (distress > support & efficacy > stress).

Not yet done

  • Repeated CV across multiple random seeds to quantify run-to-run variance.
  • Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis.
  • Modelling burnout as a continuous outcome instead of a median split.
  • VIF / ridge diagnostics for the stress–distress collinearity.
  • External validation on an independent sample.
Honest statement The near-perfect scores should be read as an upper bound produced under favourable conditions — balanced median-split classes, a modest N = 321, a single sample. Treat them as proof-of-concept until the "not yet done" column is filled in.
