A research lab on academic burnout

Can machine learning identify the psychological factors associated with academic burnout in students — and explain how it does it?

Ishaanvi Dhariwal  ·  Independent student research  ·  25 June 2026

I investigated whether academic stress, psychological distress, self-efficacy and social support could be used to predict academic burnout among secondary school (Grades 9–12) and first-year undergraduate students in India. Survey data from 321 students were used to train and compare four classifiers, evaluated with five-fold stratified cross-validation, and SHAP analysis was used to make the best model interpretable.

What the study found

Across both classical statistics and machine learning, the same three factors carried the signal. Psychological distress was the dominant predictor of burnout (mean |SHAP| = 0.231). Social support (0.163) and self-efficacy (0.110) acted as protective factors. Academic stress, despite a strong bivariate correlation with burnout, had the weakest unique contribution (0.056) once distress was in the model — it appears to work largely through emotional distress rather than independently.

A multiple linear regression told the same story: distress, self-efficacy and social support each independently predicted burnout, together explaining 58.3% of its variance (R² = .583).

Read the numbers carefully The headline classification scores are very high. That is partly a consequence of binarising burnout at the sample median — which produces two well-separated, roughly balanced classes — and of a modest sample (N = 321). These results are preliminary and proof-of-concept. The Limitations and Interpretation pages treat this directly.
